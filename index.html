Where edges exist.
And where they don’t.

Every venue that handles capital — casinos, prop firms, broker platforms, mutual funds, advisory shops — operates on the same underlying mechanic. One side of every transaction has an edge. The other side doesn’t. Over enough trials, the math wins. There is no exception to this.

Component C · The Math, Two Ways

Two operating modes. The same edge.

The same edge can be expressed in two distinct modes — depending on whether stake size is fixed or scales with equity. Our architecture runs both, simultaneously, at different layers of the system.

Linear · Casino mode

Fixed stake.
Edge × volume.

Return = edge × stake × trials

The casino’s bet size doesn’t change with the bankroll. A small edge on a fixed bet, repeated enough times, produces a predictable house take. The math accumulates rather than compounds. Our per-trade discipline runs on this principle — fixed risk per contract, no rule modification under stress, no exception across the rolling window.

Geometric · Kelly mode

Fractional stake.
The base compounds.

Capital_n = Capital_0 × exp[n × G]

When stake scales with equity, the base itself compounds. The same edge produces exponential growth instead of linear accumulation. Our scaling distribution mode (Mode B) runs on this principle — defined milestones trigger position-size doublings, the base grows, the curve bends.

Most operations sit on one mode or the other. We run both — linear at the trade level, geometric at the structural level. The Pyramid (next section) is how a real edge survives long enough to express either.

The Architecture

How the edge survives long enough to express itself.

Every edge has variance. Without a buffer engineered around it, even a real edge gets ruined by a single bad regime. The Pyramid, the Sit-Out rules, and the Falsifiability Gate exist to protect the edge — not to protect the operator’s confidence. The Buffett rule isn’t an aphorism here. It’s the engineering specification the entire defense layer is built to satisfy.

02

Layer 02  ·  The Sit-Out Architecture

Pre-committed rules. No exceptions. No renegotiation.

  • Daily  ·  1 max loss  →  stand down. Resume next session.
  • Weekly  ·  $1,000 max loss  →  stand down for the remainder of the week.
  • Edge recovery  ·  2 consecutive losing weeks  →  virtual account, full month.

The rule about the rules: the operator under loss is a different cognitive agent than the operator who set them. Under stress, the designer is gone. Only the rule remains.

Layer 03  ·  The Falsifiability Gate

How we’ve made it impossible to lie to ourselves.

Every credible system pre-defines the conditions under which it admits the edge is gone. Most trading operations do not. This one does — in writing, under witness, before founding capital is at risk.

Trigger  ·  Statistical death
Rolling 100-qualified-trade realized expectancy drops to $0 per trade — the edge crosses into net-negative territory. The 100-trade sample is sized so normal variance (≈$30 standard error) cannot trip it on a real edge.
Action  ·  Immediate stand-down
New entries drop to zero size next session. Open positions close at their next defined exit per original trade plan. Strategy moves to research-only. Public Discord notification within 24 hours, including the rolling-100 expectancy figure and the trade ID that satisfied the trigger.
Re-deploy gate  ·  Earned re-entry only
Fresh out-of-sample test of ≥ +$50 per trade over 50 qualified trades (on virtual / paper / prop-firm capital — never founding member capital)  ·  written re-derivation of the structural cause of decay  ·  48-hour cool-off after both. All three required.

Modification protocol: 48-hour cool-off before any rule change  ·  written justification (recent P&L is facially insufficient)  ·  witness countersignature required to lock  ·  public disclosure within 24 hours.

Honest caveats — what this projection assumes, and what it doesn’t
  • Sample is still inside the statistical-confirmation zone — sample-size tests in the 8-Test Sustainability Battery (below) may not clear yet at this n. Treat the annualized figure as a trajectory, not a confirmed annual expectancy.
  • Linear extrapolation assumes the edge expresses uniformly across the remaining calendar window. Regime drift, behavioral residuals (post-loss impulsivity windows, afternoon ES exposure), and seasonality are not priced in.
  • Risk scaling: the dollar projection assumes risk stays fixed at the current per-trade average. If risk scales with the equity curve (compounding), the dollar number is materially higher. If it stays fixed, the linear projection holds.
  • Profit-factor confidence interval at this sample size is meaningful — the true PF could plausibly sit anywhere from ~1.4 to ~2.1. The 8-Test Sustainability Battery quantifies that interval directly.

Principle 02

“Capped capacity preserves the edge × time product.”

Demonstrated by Renaissance Medallion Fund — closed to outside capital in 1993; ~70% gross annualized for three decades.

Never-lose foundation

Refused new outside capital at the exact moment doing so would have diluted the edge. Strict position-size and risk-budget rules. Employee-only since 1993.

Compounding engine

Small mechanical edges, executed without exception, at a size the market can absorb without degrading the edge. The math compounds because the cap was set before the temptation arrived.

Principle 03

“Risk-controlled architecture survives every regime.”

Demonstrated by Jane Street — employee-owned trading firm, profitable every year since 2000, through every crisis.

Never-lose foundation

Risk limits, position-size controls, and a capital base sized so no single event can take the firm out. Declines external capital that would dilute discipline.

Compounding engine

Small edges × massive volume × strict risk management × decades of operation. Compounding measured in firm equity, not portfolio returns.
